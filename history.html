Our History

SAGE continues a long tradition of quantitative geography at the University of Bristol.

A legacy of quantitative geography

The School of Geographical Sciences at the University of Bristol has a long and distinguished history of researching and teaching quantitative methods, spatial modelling, and geographic data science. That tradition was shaped by a series of scholars whose work helped define the field internationally.

Peter Haggett was regarded internationally as one of the most influential geographers of his generation, particularly for his work on spatial models that reshaped how geography was taught and researched worldwide. Ron Johnston was among the most widely published scholars in human geography, with a body of work spanning electoral geography, quantitative methods, and the history of the discipline. Les Hepple was known for his breadth as a scholar and teacher, with contributions spanning statistics, econometrics and the history of geographical thought. Kelvyn Jones was a pioneer of multilevel modelling in human geography, applying it to understand social and health inequalities.

From QuSS to SAGE

This tradition continued through the Quantitative Spatial Science (QuSS) research group, and now continues — in a more diverse form — through the Spatial Analysis and GeoAI (SAGE) research group.
